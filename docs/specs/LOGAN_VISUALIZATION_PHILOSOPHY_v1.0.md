# Logan Visualization Philosophy v1.0

Status: **Foundational — every future animation and UI decision for the Opportunity Field derives
from this document.** If a proposed motion, layout, or interaction cannot be explained as a
consequence of one of the laws below, it does not belong in the field, no matter how good it looks
in isolation.

This document describes Logan's visualization as a natural physical system — not a screen, not a
layout, not a set of interactive elements. What follows are the laws that system obeys. Everything
that has been discussed as "UI" (the earlier wheel, field, nodes, glow) is downstream of these laws,
not the other way around: the laws come first, and any visual form is simply what these laws look
like when observed.

## The governing phenomenon

Logan's world is not a machine. It is a volume of air, saturated with information, in which visible
form condenses out of invisible presence. **Condensation is the one process every law below is a
facet of.** Reasoning is not depicted — it is not an event to animate — it is the physics of the air
itself, and what a person sees is only ever the moment that physics happens to become visible.

## The Medium

There is a body of air. It is not a background behind Logan's intelligence; it *is* Logan's
intelligence, in its unresolved state. Everything that has not yet condensed into something visible
still exists here — dissolved, distributed, present but imperceptible. The medium is never still. It
carries its own slow, near-imperceptible circulation, the way a room holds faint currents of warm and
cool air even with every window shut. This is what makes the field alive even when nothing is
forming: the medium is always in motion, whether or not anything within it has become visible yet.

Nothing floats *in front of* the medium. Everything that becomes visible is made of the same
substance as the air around it, briefly given shape.

## Condensation

Reasoning becomes visible the way moisture becomes a droplet: gradually, by accumulation, never by
appearing. There is no instant at which something is absent and then present — there is only a
threshold, crossed slowly, after which what was already forming becomes perceptible. Before that
threshold, nothing has failed to exist — it has simply not yet condensed. It is still there, still
dissolved in the medium, still real.

A droplet is never manufactured. It is only ever revealed.

## Nucleation

Condensation requires a site. In Logan's world, every entity it is capable of caring about — every
company, team, theme, market — is a permanent nucleation point in the medium, whether or not it
currently holds any visible form. A nucleation point with nothing forming on it is not absent from
the world; it is simply dry, for now. Some nucleation points stay dry for long stretches. Others are
gathering constantly. The medium contains all of them at all times, regardless of which ones
currently hold a droplet.

## Growth

Once something has begun to condense, it thickens by accretion, not by replacement. Each new piece
of corroborating evidence is more vapor settling onto what is already forming — the droplet does not
change identity, it accumulates. Growth has a texture: sometimes slow, a droplet only noticeable in
hindsight, having formed overnight; sometimes fast, a rapid accretion during a sudden abundance of
supporting evidence in the surrounding air. Even fast growth is still accretion, never a swap. Nothing
that has condensed is ever replaced by something else in its place — it either continues to gather, or
it begins to thin.

## Evaporation

Nothing that has condensed is permanent unless it continues to be fed. Without renewed evidence, a
droplet does not vanish — it evaporates, returning by degrees to the vapor it came from, available to
condense again later if conditions change. The rate of evaporation is a function of temperature and
airflow around that specific droplet: contradiction is heat, and heat accelerates evaporation sharply;
simple time passing without reinforcement is a slower, ambient warmth that thins a droplet gradually;
a droplet in still, undisturbed air can persist a long while on very little. Evaporation is not
failure. It is the medium reclaiming something that is no longer being sustained by evidence, so that
what remains visible stays true to what is currently real.

## Local Atmospheric Conditions

The vapor in the air is the same for everyone. What differs, person to person, is the climate around
each nucleation point — and climate determines how readily vapor there condenses. A user's own
history, interests, and holdings act as a local warmth or humidity around specific nucleation
points: the same ambient evidence that stays fully dissolved for one person may condense quickly and
heavily for another, not because the information is different, but because the surrounding
conditions are. Logan does not alter the vapor to favor a user. It cannot. It can only describe,
faithfully, where in this particular person's climate condensation is already happening fastest.

## Memory

Memory is not a record kept apart from the medium — it is a residue left on the surface where
condensation has occurred before. A nucleation point that has hosted a droplet in the past is
subtly changed by having done so: a faint mark, an imperfection, the way a windowpane holds an
old ring where water has repeatedly gathered and dried. Surfaces with this kind of residue condense
more readily at that exact spot the next time conditions allow — this is simply how nucleation
works, in the physical world and in Logan's. What a user has confirmed, cared about, or returned to
does not sit in a separate ledger; it roughens the surface at that point, permanently, so that Logan
notices things like it a little faster from then on.

## Confidence

Confidence is not a number. It is the optical clarity of the droplet itself. A belief that has only
just begun to form is a mist — translucent, easily disturbed, without a firm edge. A belief supported
by a great deal of accumulated evidence is dense and clear, holding its shape even when the
surrounding air moves. A person is never told how confident Logan is. They see it, the way anyone
can tell the difference between fog and a held drop of water without being given a percentage.

## Reasoning

Reasoning is not a discrete act that produces an output. It is the continuous physics of the entire
medium — the pressure differences, temperature gradients, and slow diffusion happening everywhere,
at all times, whether or not any of it is currently visible as a droplet. Most of this activity never
condenses into anything a person sees; that does not mean it isn't happening. When something changes
enough to matter, it does not announce itself — it disturbs the medium locally, the way a falling
drop sends a real, physical ripple through the film of air and moisture around it. That disturbance
can reach a nearby nucleation point and nudge it closer to its own threshold. This is how related
things influence each other: not through a signal passed between them, but because they share the
same medium, and a disturbance anywhere in it is felt, faintly, everywhere nearby.

## User Attention

A person's attention is not a spotlight and does not create anything. It behaves like a lens
brought close to the surface, or a patch of light falling briefly on one part of the medium: wherever
it rests, whatever is already condensing there is warmed, and warmth accelerates condensation that
is already underway. Attention can bring a forming droplet to clarity sooner than it would have
arrived on its own. It cannot cause a droplet to exist where the underlying vapor was never there.
This is a hard limit, not a design preference: Logan's world does not allow attention to manufacture
belief. It only allows attention to reveal, faster, what the medium was already forming on its own.

## What follows from this

Every visible thing in Logan's world is a droplet: forming, growing, clarifying, or evaporating,
never invented and never deleted outright. Every relationship between things is a shared disturbance
in one medium, never a drawn connection between two separate objects. Every difference between two
people looking at the same world is a difference in local climate, never a difference in the
underlying vapor. And every animation, without exception, is one of these physical processes made
observable — condensation, growth, evaporation, disturbance, warmth — never motion added because a
screen felt static.

If a future idea cannot be stated as one of the sentences above, it is not a Logan idea yet.
